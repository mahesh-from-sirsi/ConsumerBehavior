Case Study Library

Read each case, attempt the questions first, then expand the suggested analysis.

Case 1: The 5,000-Follower Local Creator

A Shivamogga café has ₹10,000 to promote a new student combo. Creator A has 100,000 followers spread across India with 1% average engagement. Creator B has 5,000 mostly local followers with 9% engagement. No verified sales data is available.

Discuss: Which creator is likely to be more relevant and why? What evidence must be checked before paying? How will you measure results?

Show suggested analysis

B is a plausible first test because local audience fit matters, but engagement alone does not establish sales impact. Check audience geography, authenticity, content fit and fee. Use unique codes, redemptions and incremental sales.

Case 2: A Family Buys a Refrigerator

A household needs a refrigerator. A teenager identifies the problem, one partner researches efficiency ratings, another checks service centres, and grandparents care about ease of use. The final choice is discussed jointly.

Discuss: Identify initiator, influencers, buyer, payer, user and decider. Which concerns should the retailer address?

Show suggested analysis

Roles can overlap; the teenager is initiator, researchers and grandparents influence, and the decider may be joint. Ask who pays and purchases rather than assuming. Demonstrate efficiency, service and accessibility.

Case 3: A New UPI Feature

A local cooperative launches QR payments for a weekly market. Early users report convenience; some merchants fear failed payments and scams. Adoption grows after demonstrations and a helpline.

Discuss: Identify innovation attributes, adopter groups and adoption stages. Propose a responsible diffusion plan.

Show suggested analysis

Relative advantage and observability help; perceived complexity and risk hinder. Offer low-risk trials, verified demonstrations, dispute support and fraud education; track active use rather than registration.

Case 4: Who Is the Expert?

An MBA class asks one student for phone advice, another for investment basics, and a third for where to find affordable products. A brand wants a single campus ambassador for all three categories.

Discuss: Distinguish opinion leader from market maven. Is one ambassador sufficient?

Show suggested analysis

Expertise is often category-specific; the broad bargain-information sharer may be a market maven. Select by domain credibility and avoid presenting unqualified financial advice.

Case 5: Family Life Cycle Without Stereotypes

A 45-year-old single professional supports parents, a 29-year-old married couple has no children, and a multigenerational household shares one budget. A retailer segments them solely by traditional FLC labels.

Discuss: What does the retailer miss? Design a better segmentation approach.

Show suggested analysis

Traditional FLC may miss care duties, preferences, income and household structures. Segment by actual needs, spending authority, living arrangements and observed behaviour.
