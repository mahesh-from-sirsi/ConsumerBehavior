Case studies

Tata Nano: price and perceived image

Adapted from Module 3 detailed notes

A low-priced car is promoted as an economical mobility solution. Some buyers may interpret a “cheap car” message as a signal about status or quality, even when affordability is useful.

Discussion questions

  1. Which perceptual factors are relevant?
  2. How could marketers reposition the offer without misleading customers?
Reveal suggested discussion points
  1. Perceived quality, social risk, brand image and selective interpretation matter.
  2. Emphasise functional value, safety evidence and convenience rather than price alone.

Fevicol: learning brand associations

Adapted from Module 3 detailed notes

Repeated memorable advertising links a brand of adhesive with the idea of unusually strong bonding.

Discussion questions

  1. Which learning principles could explain recall?
  2. What evidence would distinguish brand recall from actual purchase?
Reveal suggested discussion points
  1. Repetition and association can strengthen memory and recognition.
  2. Measure unaided recall separately from consideration, trial and repeat purchase.

Royal Enfield: personality and self-concept

Adapted from Module 3 detailed notes

Some buyers see a motorcycle brand as representing independence, heritage or adventure, not merely transport.

Discussion questions

  1. How do brand personality and ideal self-image interact?
  2. Why might this appeal vary by consumer?
Reveal suggested discussion points
  1. Symbolic attributes may fit an aspirational identity.
  2. Lifestyle, budget, experience and practical requirements vary.
