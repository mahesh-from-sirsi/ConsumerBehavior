Case studies

C.G. Electronics: the showroom assumption

Adapted from Module 1 detailed notes

A home-appliance company invests in exclusive brand showrooms, assuming customers prefer a dedicated brand experience. Sales disappoint. Consumer research finds that many buyers prefer multi-brand stores because they can compare alternatives before making a high-value purchase.

Discussion questions

  1. What assumption did management make about the buying process?
  2. Which consumer-research methods could test the preference for multi-brand outlets?
  3. What changes to distribution and communication would you recommend?
Reveal suggested discussion points
  1. The firm assumed that a dedicated showroom would outweigh comparison convenience.
  2. Use shopper interviews, store intercepts and observation, with a sample covering different buyer segments.
  3. Test multi-brand distribution and comparison-friendly product information before expanding outlets.

Who buys and who consumes?

Instructional scenario based on Module 1 concepts

A parent pays for a laptop, the MBA student selects the specifications, and a sibling recommends the brand. The student is its main user.

Discussion questions

  1. Identify payer, influencer, decider, buyer and user.
  2. Who should a laptop brand address in its advertising?
Reveal suggested discussion points
  1. The parent pays; the sibling influences; the student helps decide and uses it; the actual buyer depends on who places the order.
  2. Messages should address the student’s needs and the parent’s concerns about value and reliability.
