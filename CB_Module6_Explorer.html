1. CRM Concepts, Definition and Customer Acquisition

CRM meaning

Customer Relationship Management (CRM) is a customer-centred strategy and set of processes, people practices, data and technologies for acquiring, serving, retaining and developing profitable, mutually valuable customer relationships. CRM is not merely contact software or a discount scheme.

Operational, analytical and collaborative CRM

Operational CRM supports sales pipelines, marketing campaigns and service tickets. Analytical CRM uses data for segmentation, forecasting and measurement. Collaborative CRM coordinates customer information and actions across sales, marketing, service and authorised partners.

Customer lifecycle

A practical lifecycle is awareness, lead, qualified prospect, first purchase, onboarding, repeat use, loyalty, advocacy or lapse. Movement is not always linear: a customer can complain, pause and return.

Customer acquisition process

Define the ideal customer profile and problem; identify channels; generate leads; qualify need, budget and fit; communicate value; convert with informed consent; onboard; measure conversion quality and acquisition cost.

Customer acquisition cost (CAC)

CAC = attributable acquisition sales-and-marketing cost divided by number of newly acquired customers for the same period and attribution rules. Example: Rs. 60,000 spent to acquire 30 customers gives Rs. 2,000 CAC. State whether staff costs, promotions and agency fees are included.

Acquisition versus retention

A campaign producing cheap sign-ups is not necessarily successful if customers churn quickly. Track activation, complaints, repeat purchase, contribution margin and consent quality. Acquisition and retention investments must be assessed together.

Indian illustration

A Shivamogga appliance retailer records consented enquiries from walk-ins and WhatsApp, follows up on warranty queries, and measures the percentage that become paying customers rather than celebrating raw contact counts.

2. Customer Loyalty, Relationship Optimisation and CRM Success Factors

Satisfaction, loyalty and retention

Satisfaction is a judgement about an experience; behavioural loyalty is repeated patronage; attitudinal loyalty is preference and commitment; retention is the share of existing customers remaining active under a defined rule. Repeated purchase can also reflect convenience or switching barriers.

Relationship optimisation

Prioritise value for both customer and business through relevant offers, service recovery, appropriate contact frequency, transparent policies and lifecycle-based support. Avoid contacting customers solely because their data exists.

Loyalty programme design

Set a clear customer benefit, simple eligibility, fair redemption rules, cost control and safeguards against reward abuse. Measure incremental repeat purchases and margin, not only enrolments.

CRM success factors

Leadership commitment; clear customer strategy; trained employees; trustworthy and permissioned data; process ownership; integration with billing and support; adoption by frontline teams; useful KPIs; privacy and security; ongoing testing and improvement.

Common reasons for failure

Poor data quality, unclear ownership, aggressive message frequency, incentives rewarding quantity rather than customer outcomes, fragmented service histories and technology installed without workflow redesign.

Mini example

A clinic sends appointment reminders only to patients who consented, offers rescheduling, and checks whether no-shows decline. Better service is a relationship outcome; the reminder itself is not proof of loyalty.

3. Three Levels of Service/Sales Profiling and SLAs

Interpretation note

The syllabus names three levels of Service/Sales Profiling without defining a universal model. For classroom use, these notes adopt a transparent operational framework: basic descriptive profile, behavioural/needs profile, and relationship/value profile. This is an instructional interpretation, not a mandated VTU taxonomy.

Level 1: descriptive

Capture permitted contact details, product held, purchase date, location, preferred language and service eligibility. Example: warranty status for a mixer grinder.

Level 2: behavioural and needs

Use observed purchase frequency, enquiry history, complaint category, channel preference and expressed needs. Example: customer prefers text updates and asks for repair instead of replacement.

Level 3: relationship and value

Combine customer journey stage, service complexity, lifetime contribution estimates, churn risk and relationship objectives. Example: proactive onboarding for a high-complexity business customer; do not deny essential service to low-value customers.

SLA definition

A Service Level Agreement (SLA) documents service commitments between provider and customer or internal parties, including scope, responsibilities, measurable targets, measurement period, exclusions, escalation and remedies.

SLA metrics

First-response time, resolution time, uptime, fulfilment time, first-contact resolution, abandonment rate and quality measures. Specify business hours, clock start/stop rules and severity categories.

Worked SLA

For priority-1 service outage: acknowledgement within 30 minutes, updates every 60 minutes, target restoration within 4 hours during contracted coverage. A target is a commitment to measure and manage, not a guarantee that every incident will meet it.

How to manage SLAs

Agree customer expectations; segment incident severity; define metric formulas and owners; record timestamps; publish dashboards; investigate breaches; communicate recovery; revise unrealistic targets with evidence.

SLA versus KPI

An SLA is a documented service commitment. A KPI is an indicator used to monitor performance. A KPI can measure an SLA, but many KPIs are not contractual commitments.

4. CRM in Marketing and One-to-One Relationship Marketing

CRM in marketing

CRM links campaign activity to customer needs, purchase history, channel preference, customer service and measured outcomes. It supports segmentation, personalisation, lifecycle journeys and suppression of irrelevant contact.

One-to-one relationship marketing

Tailoring communications, recommendations or service to an individual based on permissioned information and stated needs. True one-to-one marketing requires meaningful relevance, not merely inserting a first name into a bulk message.

Segmentation versus personalisation

Segmentation groups customers with similar needs; personalisation adapts experience to an individual. A tier-two college can begin with needs-based segments before implementing complex AI.

RFM analysis

Recency = time since last purchase; Frequency = purchases in a defined period; Monetary = amount spent in that period. Use consistent windows and avoid assuming high spend always means high profitability.

Lifecycle campaigns

Welcome series after purchase, usage guidance, service reminders, renewal prompts and reactivation campaigns. Each needs a trigger, consent basis, message, frequency cap and success metric.

Ethical personalisation

Collect the minimum necessary data, explain purpose, respect opt-outs and avoid inferring sensitive attributes. In India, assess applicable privacy requirements and company policies before collecting or using personal data.

5. Cross-Selling, Up-Selling and Customer Retention

Cross-selling

Offering a relevant complementary product or service: a laptop buyer may benefit from a protective sleeve or legitimate extended support. The offer must be appropriate and not misleading.

Up-selling

Offering a higher-specification or higher-value version of the product under consideration: 16 GB RAM rather than 8 GB where workload justifies it. Higher price alone does not mean higher customer value.

Cross-selling versus bundling

Cross-selling proposes complementary items; bundling packages items together, sometimes with a price incentive. A bundle can include unnecessary items and should be evaluated for fairness.

Retention levers

Reliable fulfilment, effective onboarding, service recovery, product quality, transparent renewal, appropriate rewards and convenient support. Discounting alone may train customers to wait for offers.

Retention rate

Customer retention rate = (customers at period end minus new customers acquired during period) / customers at period start × 100, using a consistent active-customer definition. Example: start 200, end 230, new 50 => 90% retention.

Churn and cohorts

Churn measures customers who leave under a stated definition. Cohort analysis compares customers acquired in the same month to separate lifecycle effects from seasonal demand.

Practical scenario

A two-wheeler dealership offers annual service reminders and transparent maintenance estimates; cross-sells helmets only when relevant, and upsells accessories only with clear benefits and prices.

6. Behaviour Prediction and Customer Profitability / Value Modelling

Prediction tasks

Predict probability of repeat purchase, churn, response to a relevant offer, service demand or likely product needs. Predictions are uncertain estimates, not facts about an individual.

Signals and data quality

Purchase recency, frequency, usage, complaint history and service delays can be useful when lawful and accurate. Missing records, seasonality, biased samples and changes in customer circumstances can reduce accuracy.

Simple churn-risk score

Illustrative rule: 2 points if no purchase in 90 days, 1 if unresolved complaint, 1 if previous renewal was late. A score of 3+ triggers a service check, not an automatic aggressive sales campaign. Validate against outcomes.

Customer profitability

Customer contribution = customer revenue minus attributable product, fulfilment, service, discounts and other variable costs over a stated period. Allocated overhead may be added for managerial analysis, but allocation assumptions must be disclosed.

Customer lifetime value (CLV)

CLV estimates future customer contribution over a defined horizon, usually discounted for time and adjusted for retention. Simple classroom approximation: annual contribution × expected relationship years minus acquisition cost. Example: Rs. 3,000 × 4 − Rs. 2,000 = Rs. 10,000 before discounting and uncertainty.

Profitability versus value

A customer may be low-profit now but have future potential, referral value or strategic importance. Do not confuse predicted CLV with guaranteed revenue or use it to justify unfair treatment.

Validation and fairness

Use holdout data, compare prediction with a simple baseline, monitor false positives and drift, and review unintended discrimination. Respect consent and applicable data protection obligations.

7. Channel Optimisation and Event-Based Marketing

Channels

Store, field sales, website, app, email, SMS, WhatsApp, telephone and authorised marketplaces. Each differs in reach, cost, urgency, accessibility and customer preference.

Channel optimisation

Choose the right channel and timing for a customer need using cost per successful outcome, response, resolution quality, satisfaction and opt-out rates. Do not optimise only for cheapest contact.

Omnichannel continuity

With appropriate access controls, service agents should see relevant prior interactions so customers need not repeat their issue. A common customer ID and accurate ticket handoffs are more important than flashy dashboards.

Event-based marketing

A message or action triggered by a meaningful event: first purchase, cart abandonment with consent, warranty expiry, renewal date, complaint closure, milestone or usage change.

Trigger design

Define event, audience, eligibility, message, channel, delay, suppression, frequency cap, success metric and failure handling. Avoid sensitive life events unless specifically appropriate and lawful.

Example

A water-purifier seller sends a filter-replacement reminder after the recommended interval, with a service booking link. Measure completed service bookings, opt-outs and complaint rates.

Attribution caveat

Customers who respond to a campaign may have purchased anyway. Compare against a suitable holdout or control group when possible to estimate incremental effect.

8. CRM and Customer Service: Call Centres and Call Scripting

Call centre role

Call centres handle inbound questions, complaints, orders and outbound permitted service or sales calls. Contact centres extend this to chat, email, messaging and other channels.

Inbound versus outbound

Inbound customers initiate contact; outbound agents contact customers for service reminders, research or consented sales. Compliance, purpose and customer preference differ.

Call centre measures

Average speed of answer, abandonment rate, average handling time, first-contact resolution, quality assurance, repeat-contact rate and customer satisfaction. Faster calls are not automatically better calls.

Call script structure

Greeting and identity; consent/verification appropriate to risk; listen and clarify; empathise without making false promises; diagnose; explain options; confirm next steps, ownership and reference number; close courteously.

Sample complaint script

Agent: “Thank you for calling. I am sorry your mixer stopped working. May I confirm the purchase date and the issue?” Customer explains. Agent: “I can arrange an inspection or explain the warranty claim process. Which works for you?” Agent confirms timeline and ticket ID.

Escalation

Escalate safety concerns, repeated failures, financial disputes and potential privacy incidents according to documented rules. Preserve notes and inform customers of expected updates.

Service recovery

Acknowledge the failure, take ownership, provide a realistic remedy, follow up and learn from root causes. A scripted apology without action can reduce trust.

9. Customer Satisfaction Measurement and CRM Dashboard

CSAT

Customer Satisfaction Score asks customers to rate a specific experience, often on a five-point scale. If top-two-box is defined as 4 or 5, CSAT = (number of 4 or 5 responses / valid responses) × 100. Example: 72 satisfied of 90 responses => 80%.

NPS

Net Promoter Score asks likelihood to recommend on 0–10. Promoters = 9–10, passives = 7–8, detractors = 0–6. NPS = percentage promoters minus percentage detractors; it ranges from −100 to +100. It is not a direct measure of retention or profitability.

CES

Customer Effort Score measures perceived ease of completing a task or resolving an issue. Wording and scale vary, so disclose the exact question and calculation.

Survey design

Ask about a defined experience promptly, use neutral language, make participation voluntary, offer accessible channels, protect respondent privacy and report sample size and nonresponse.

Example dashboard

For a monthly service dashboard: new customers, CAC, repeat purchase rate, cohort retention, SLA compliance, first-contact resolution, CSAT, complaints per 100 orders and opt-out rate.

Interpretation pitfalls

High CSAT can coexist with high churn; NPS can be skewed by nonresponse; SLA compliance can mask poor resolutions. Triangulate operational, financial and customer-reported measures.

Managerial action

Define metric owner, threshold, review frequency and corrective action. Example: if repeat complaints increase, review repair quality before investing in more acquisition campaigns.
